# Background rates of neighbor-contact chemistry (PDB survey)

Generated by `scripts/survey_neighbor_chemistry.py` for TODO item D. 2048 atomic-resolution X-ray structures (one per 30% sequence cluster, 1.2 A or better, first chain); 346827 pairs of residues one apart in sequence and joined by a peptide bond. Classes are those of `residue_contacts(neighbor_contacts=True, neighbor_chemistry=NEIGHBOR_CHEMISTRY_ALL)`: an atom pair at least 4 bonds apart through the peptide bond that is `polar` (a hydrogen bond with one side-chain atom: donor to acceptor within 3.5 A, and where the donor's hydrogen can be placed it must point at the acceptor), `ionic`, `hydrophobic` (C/S pair, both atoms beyond C-beta, within 4.0 A) or `aromatic` (a ring atom against a side-chain atom beyond C-beta, within 4.0 A).

## What the neighbor classes leave out, and how much

- C-beta against C-beta: the C-beta atoms of neighbors are within 4.0 A in 0.01% of 292200 pairs and within 4.5 A in 0.19%, so leaving C-beta out of the hydrophobic class costs almost nothing and is a principle (the C-beta position follows from the backbone), not a large correction.
- A ring atom within 4.0 A of the neighbor's backbone or C-beta (at least 4 bonds apart): 32594 residue pairs, 43.0% of the 75732 neighbor pairs that include a Phe, Tyr, Trp or His; the `aromatic` class (ring against a side-chain atom beyond C-beta) fires in 6849 pairs. A ring sits beside the next backbone almost by construction, so counting that adjacency would mark every aromatic residue; the classes count only ring-to-side-chain contacts.

How to use it: a conserved neighbor contact of a residue-type pair whose background rate is high (for example Ile followed by Pro, 47%, for hydrophobic) says little; one with a low background rate is unusual. The per-pair table is `06_neighbor_chemistry_background.csv`.

Caveats: atomic-resolution structures are small, well-ordered proteins; one chain per cluster; first model.
